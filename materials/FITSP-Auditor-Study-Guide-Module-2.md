# FITSP-Auditor Study Guide: Module 2, Federal Laws

Covers student book pages 27, 28, 30 and 31. Page 29 was not in the scan.

## At a glance

The whole module hangs on one chain of authority:

1. The President sets an agenda (the President's Management Agenda, or PMA).
2. Congress passes laws that support it.
3. Those laws hand oversight or authority to specific bodies: OMB, DHS, HHS, NIST and CNSS.
4. Those bodies issue the policies, standards and guidance that federal agencies must follow.
5. Auditors check agencies against that guidance.

If you can say which law gave which body its job, you can answer most questions from these pages.

## Who got what authority

Each law below gave one body a specific job. Learn the three columns as a set.

| Law | Gave the job to | What came out of it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paperwork Reduction Act (PRA) | OMB | Government-wide policy and oversight; OMB Circular A-130 |
| Homeland Security Act (HSA) | DHS | Created DHS and gave it authority to protect the homeland |
| HIPAA | HHS (the Secretary) | The HIPAA Security Rule for health information |
| HITECH (part of ARRA 2009) | HHS | Stronger enforcement of the Privacy Rule and Security Rule |
| Computer Security Act (CSA) and FISMA | NIST and NSA | Standards and guidelines; NIST built the Risk Management Framework (RMF) |

Five more facts from these pages:

- **Liaisons.** In 2009 President Obama created two staff roles: the Federal CIO (liaison to OMB) and the Cybersecurity Coordinator (liaison to DHS).
- **National security systems.** These follow CNSS guidance. CNSSI No. 1253 supports CNSS Policy 22 (the Information Assurance Risk Management Policy) and National Security Directive 42.
- **FISMA 2002.** It required every agency to run an information security program, assessed each year by the agency Inspector General. IT auditors do most of that assessment work.
- **FISMA 2014.** It replaced most, but not all, of FISMA 2002.
- **Mandatory or not.** FIPS are mandatory for federal agencies. Other NIST guidance is partly mandatory and partly recommended.

The two PMAs named: President Bush published the first in 2001. President Trump's, in March 2018, had three areas: IT Modernization; Data, Accountability and Transparency; and the Workforce of the Future. Just before it, Congress passed the Modernizing Government Technology Act of 2017 inside the 2018 National Defense Authorization Act (Title X, Subtitle G).

## Memory aids

- **A-130 always follows a law.** Every revision trails the law it absorbs: PRA 1980 → 1985, CSA 1987 → 1993, PRA 1995 → 1996, Clinger-Cohen 1996 → 2000, FISMA 2014 → 2016. If a question asks why A-130 changed, look for the law just before it.
- **FISMA arrived twice.** November 2002 in the Homeland Security Act, December 2002 in the E-Government Act. Alphabetical order does not help here, so remember "Homeland first."
- **199 before 200.** Categorize the system (FIPS 199, 2004), then apply minimum requirements (FIPS 200, 2006). The numbers and the dates run in the same order as the work.
- **GISRA → FISMA 2002 → FISMA 2014.** Each one replaced the one before, and the last replaced "most, not all."
- **Clinger-Cohen is two acts.** ITMRA plus the Federal Acquisition Reform Act, both 1996.
- **Two executive orders.** 13636 (2013) is critical infrastructure only. 13800 (2017) adds federal networks.
- **December 2014 is the busy month.** Four laws: Cybersecurity Enhancement, National Cybersecurity Protection, FITARA, FISMA 2014.
- **Pairs of bodies.** OMB oversees, DHS has operational authority, NIST writes guidance for most systems, CNSS writes it for national security systems, HHS covers health information.

### Two things to watch in the book

- Page 30's body text expands FISMA as "Federal Information Systems Management Act" and "Systems Modernization Act." The tables on pages 30 and 31, and the law itself, say **Security**: Federal Information Security Management Act (2002) and Federal Information Security Modernization Act (2014).
- The table lists the Computer Security Act as "PL 100-35." The actual public law number is 100-235. If the exam asks, the answer choices should make the intended one obvious.

## Self-quiz

Cover the answers, say yours out loud, then check. Use this as the last pomodoro of the session.

1. Which law gave OMB responsibility for government-wide policy? **Paperwork Reduction Act.**
2. Which was the first law from Congress to address computer security, and what did it task NIST to do? **Computer Security Act of 1987; provide standards and guidelines for computer security.**
3. FISMA 2002 was included in which two acts, and in which order? **Homeland Security Act (Nov 2002), then E-Government Act (Dec 2002).**
4. Who performs the annual assessment of an agency's security program under FISMA? **The agency Inspector General.**
5. What did FISMA replace? **GISRA (2000).**
6. Did FISMA 2014 replace all of FISMA 2002? **No. Most, but not all.**
7. What is FIPS 199, and what is FIPS 200? **Categorization (2004); minimum security requirements (2006).**
8. Are FIPS mandatory for federal agencies? **Yes.**
9. Why was OMB Circular A-130 first published, and when was it last revised in the book? **To implement the PRA of 1980; July 2016, due to FISMA 2014.**
10. Which two acts make up Clinger-Cohen? **ITMRA and the Federal Acquisition Reform Act, both 1996.**
11. Which law produced the HIPAA Security Rule, and who had to adopt it? **HIPAA; the Secretary of HHS.**
12. What did HITECH do, and what larger act was it part of? **Mandated better enforcement of the Privacy and Security Rules; ARRA 2009.**
13. Which two positions did President Obama create in 2009, and which body is each a liaison to? **Federal CIO → OMB; Cybersecurity Coordinator → DHS.**
14. Which instruction covers national security systems, and which policy and directive does it support? **CNSSI No. 1253; CNSS Policy 22 and National Security Directive 42.**
15. Name the three focus areas of the March 2018 PMA. **IT Modernization; Data, Accountability and Transparency; Workforce of the Future.**
16. Where was the Modernizing Government Technology Act passed? **Title X, Subtitle G of the 2018 NDAA.**
17. What is the difference between EO 13636 and EO 13800? **13636 (2013) covers critical infrastructure; 13800 (2017) covers federal networks and critical infrastructure.**
18. Name the four laws passed in December 2014. **Cybersecurity Enhancement Act, National Cybersecurity Protection Act, FITARA, FISMA 2014.**
